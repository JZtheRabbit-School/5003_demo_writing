Draft · Personal essay

The shape of a quiet morning

by Eliana Brooks · Submitted today
Editor mode is on. Select any passage to leave a specific note, or add your overall feedback at the end.

Before the city has made up its mind about the day, there is a small interval of mercy. The apartment is still, except for the radiator clicking awake and the kettle beginning its slow argument with the stove.

I have come to trust this hour more than any other. It asks nothing of me but attention: the blue cast of early light on the counter, the neighbor's dog shaking sleep from its collar, the one clean page waiting beside my cup.

My grandmother called this “the borrowed time.” She rose before everyone else to sit at her kitchen table, hands wrapped around a mug, looking out at the yard. I never asked what she was thinking. Now I suspect that was the point.

Later, messages arrive and the day becomes a set of doors opening at once. But for a few minutes, the world is only a room, a window, and the soft work of beginning.
